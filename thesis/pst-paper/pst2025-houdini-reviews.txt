----------- Overall evaluation -----------
SCORE: 1 (weak accept)
----- TEXT:
Summary: This paper presents and evaluates a technique called Houdini, that verifies container escapes are prevented. 



Strengths: 
+ Novel idea 
+ Adequate evaluation 

Weaknesses: 
- Certain methodological choices can be clarified 

Details: 

The paper targets a relevant problem, where Houdini is presented for verifying container confinement. 
Houdini relies on identifying misconfigurations in Dockerfiles that can lead to container escapes. The applied technique 
is novel in the context of Dockerfiles. The paper also discuss three case studies each of which show the viability of the testing framework available as part of Houdini. The case studies show that Houdini can detect misconfigurations that can enable container escapes. 

While the reviewer is positive about the paper, the authors can improve on the methodological choices for the paper. For example, the authors can clarify the following: 

- What was the reason behind selecting the QEMU VM? 
- What was the process of generating the tricks, i.e., the test cases? 
- Was there a specific reason on selecting the first case study to be related with vulnerabilities? 
- What were the reasons for motivating the selection of the five configuration parameters listed in page#6

AUTHORS: Huzaifa Patel, David Barrera and Anil Somayaji

----------- Overall evaluation -----------
SCORE: 1 (weak accept)
----- TEXT:
The paper addresses a key problem in cloud instrastructure: container isolation. The topic is tricky, because the desired isolation level depends on the use case and the needs for the end-application. That is why the authors focus on the baseline configuration and tests to assess that security posture.

I argue that Hudini whould assess the *maximum* set of privileges, that is to say, if adding more privileges, it expands the attach surface of the container. Least privilege is essential. 

The exemplary applications of Hudini cover most of use cases at T=O. As future work (and implied in the paper), continuous verification is a must. Configurations are dynamic, change through time, policies are updated. Hoduini needs a way to adapt to new requirements.
Likewise, verify how this approach aligns with NESAS/SCAS standards for assurance. By aligning with a standard, will make this proposition even stronger.



----------------------- REVIEW 3 ---------------------
SUBMISSION: 76
TITLE: Houdini: Benchmarking Container Security Confinement
AUTHORS: Huzaifa Patel, David Barrera and Anil Somayaji

----------- Overall evaluation -----------
SCORE: 2 (accept)
----- TEXT:
Houdini is a powerful tool that can be used to assess the security posture of containers, especially docker containers. The fact that it can be integrated into a CI/CD pipeline is a big plus. The authors have evaluated Houdini against various CVEs and have proven its effectiveness. 

However, the authors have limited themselves to containers and have not explored container orchestration technologies. If Houdini can find vulnerabilities in a containerized environment it stands to reason that it can also be applied to an entire cluster of containers. It would be interesting to see Houdini extended to benchmark a Kubernetes cluster. Also, compatibility with other container environments can be explored. If the approach is generic enough, proof that it can be applied to any contain tech can be useful. 

The first contribution of the paper, “Defines container confinement and its associated challenges” cannot be counted as new and interesting. The definition and the challenges are drawn from the literature. Moreover, there are many static analysis tools that uncover vulnerabilities in the configuration files. For instance, trivy, kubescape etc. I understand that static analysis tools do not perform dynamic testing which is possible with Houdini but the advantage of such testing is unclear. A comparison between the results of such tools and Houdini would make the need for Houdini stand out. 

There are certain minor issues that should be fixed. For instance, the bottom portion of Figure 1 is cut.
