\chapter*{Abstract}
\fontsize{12pt}{14pt} \selectfont
\begin{doublespace}
Containers have become a foundational technology in cloud computing due to their speed, lightweight nature, and ease of deployment. However, ensuring they are truly secure and properly isolated remains a challenge. Most tools focus on scanning images for vulnerabilities or validating configurations against best practices. While useful, these approaches do not confirm whether confinement mechanisms such as namespaces, cgroups, seccomp, or AppArmor are enforced at runtime. As a result, containers may appear secure on paper yet still expose gaps due to misconfigurations, version-specific behaviors, silent enforcement failures, or incomplete policies.

To address this, this thesis presents Houdini, a modular framework for empirically testing container confinement. Houdini executes scripted tests, called tricks, inside an isolated virtual machine to observe whether features like namespaces, cgroups, seccomp, and AppArmor are enforced at runtime. By simulating container actions, Houdini reveals when isolation mechanisms silently fail.

Through case studies, Houdini highlights misconfigurations, enforcement failures, and inconsistencies across combinations of Docker, runc, and Linux kernel versions. These studies show how seemingly secure containers can violate isolation guarantees due to subtle interactions between runtime settings and system versions. By making container confinement observable and testable, Houdini provides a practical foundation for building and validating more secure container systems.

\end{doublespace}
